01 · The problem
Signal Reframing the problem

eBay couldn't hear "near me."

People trying to buy something bulky, urgent, or see-it-first can't use eBay locally. eBay hears "couch" and ignores "near me, pick up Saturday," so they trade eBay's protection for Facebook Marketplace's proximity.

On the other side, a seller with a 140-pound sectional faces $120+ of freight or a stranger in the driveway with no proof of handoff.

couch near me, pick up Saturday
Sectional couch · ships from 800 miles away
+ $120 freight shipping
So the buyer gives up, and goes to Facebook Marketplace.
The reframe
Not a search bug. A missing brain.

eBay already had the parts: pickup filters, QR codes, pay at pickup. Nothing connected them. Nothing heard "near me," judged how sure it was, and did the legwork that kills local deals after the click.

That made this an agent project, not a ranking project.
Say: Every week, buyers tell us exactly what they want. "Couch near me, pick up Saturday." We hear "couch," and we send them a sectional from 800 miles away with $120 of freight. So they leave, and they buy it on Facebook. I'm going to show you the agent that fixes that, on both sides of the deal, the numbers from the first cities, and what it needs next.

05 · The playbook
Signal Decision-making

When does the agent jump in?

Two dead ends, and the one that shipped.

A · Killed
Always push local

Feels pushy. People learn to ignore the module, and then they ignore us. In the prototype the home shelf is opt-in: "Show nearby finds / Maybe later."

B · Killed
Always ask first

You typed "pick up Saturday," so why is it asking? Asking about what you just said reads as deafness. Instead the agent plays back "Here's how I read that" and searches.

C · Shipped
Match the buyer's energy

Said it clearly → act.
Maybe → a gentle offer.
Didn't mean it → nothing.

"USB-C cable" → score 0.08 → zero local behavior Knowing when to do nothing is half the design.
ACT
When it's sure and a mistake is cheap. Always leaves an undo note.
ASK
When it's unsure, or the next step can't be undone. Always skippable.
SHOW
A short shelf, each pick labeled with what's off.
EDUCATE
One tip, at the exact moment it helps. Never a lecture.
The rule that never bends
If it can't be undone (a trip, a payment, a meetup), the agent asks first. Every time. At any confidence.
It never spends a person's money on its own.

12 · What I'd do differently, and the ask
Signal Honest judgment

"I built the buyer's side first, and left the seller waiting."

The honest regret.

I designed the buyer's side to near-perfection before giving the seller the same depth. If nobody lists their couch, the best couch-finder finds nothing. Getting the order wrong cost us a quarter.

What changed in my practice.

Both sides of a marketplace now get designed in the first document, not the third. The seller loop you just saw is the correction.

The ask
Fund the seller-side rollout in the three test cities.

Q&A prep · in my pocket

The questions I expect.

How much did this cost, and what's the return?

Early cities show +14% local sales vs. control, directional. The three-city test is the number I'd want before we scale, and I'd rather give you that one than a projection. [Team size, quarters, GMV if available.]

Does local cannibalize shipped GMV?

The categories that move are the ones we structurally lose today: furniture, appliances, fitness. The 41% who leave mid-search aren't buying a shipped couch from us. Recovered demand, not shifted demand.

What's our liability when a handoff goes wrong?

The agent never books a trip, sets a meetup, or moves money on its own. The pass releases payment only on the seller's scan. Inspection adjustments are agreed before money moves. Trust's no-wasted-trips rule is a hard guardrail.

Why will sellers list here instead of Facebook?

Instant payout on a verified handoff, neighborhood-only privacy until a time is set, a request queue ranked by show rate, and a backup buyer who's auto-notified. Selling here has to be less work than Facebook.

Why an agent and not better filters?

We ran the trace. Buyers never got far enough to see a filter. The failure is that the system hears "couch" and ignores the rest, then stops caring at the click.

Pay at pickup: doesn't that undo the protection?

It's a choice, and it's labeled: pay now and you're covered by the Money Back Guarantee; pay when you meet and the scan still closes the order and marks it paid. The fee is invoiced either way.

How does this become AI Mode?

It's the Nearby Now task. Same playbook, same response policy. [Confirm status and your role.]

What don't we know yet?

Whether people skip eBay for local because they can't find it or because they don't believe we do local. Three cities, now.
