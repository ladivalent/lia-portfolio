eBay AI Mode

Redesign search → Two small signals became an agent that asks first

We set out to redesign search, and it ended somewhere bigger. Shoppers kept typing a size or the words “near me,” and search treated both as noise. I led the work that turned those two signals into one confidence rule, verified seller data and a 12-team personalization effort. That foundation became AI Mode: an agent that acts when it's sure, asks once when it isn't, and never moves money on its own.

Three rules I work by
01
Treat a repeat as a signal.

If the same “noise” shows up every year, it's a use case hiding in the data, not an edge case to smooth over.

02
Fix it where the data starts.

A clean buyer experience can't be bolted on at search time. It has to start at the listing, with the seller.

03
Go deep, then make it simple.

You go deep into the breadth of a problem before you can bring back something simple, like one chip that asks one question.

01 · Where it started
Signal Framing the brief

Small fixes, compounding effects.

The brief looked modest: take the traditional parts of search and give them a modern spin. I broke it into three levers, each a small shift with a long tail.

1
How it's organized

Group results by what the shopper is deciding between, not by how the catalog is stored.

2
How it's laid out

A page with rhythm: density for scanning, and room where a choice needs it.

3
How it's ranked

Reward listings that earn trust, so the right match rises, not the loudest one.

02 · The signal
Signal Reframing the problem

The glitch that wasn't a glitch.

Two kinds of queries kept spiking in the same places every year: shoppers typing a size, and shoppers typing “near me” or “pick up Saturday.” Search treated both as noise, because neither fit how results were organized. My read was different: if it keeps happening, there's a reason.

Size
#1
Size and fit is the top reason online apparel gets returned, cited by 53% of apparel decision-makers.
Size went in as free text and came out as free text. The seller knew which 12 they meant. The platform didn't.
Coresight Research, 2023
Local intent
11%
of 500 traced searches with local words ended in a local pickup.
41% left for another app mid-search, 27% left at the shipping price, and 21% got stuck in filters.
Each spike was a use case hiding in the data. Not an edge case to smooth over, but a door to a new mode of search.
03 · Worked example 1 · Size
Signal Designing for ambiguity

Size 8 has eight meanings.

A shopper types one number. Before search can filter, it has to know the category, gender, fit and country behind it. Size isn't a value. It's a token that has to be read in context.

8
one input
US women's clothing
US men's shoe
UK apparel
EU 38, mapped
JP 24.5 cm shoe
Brand A ≠ Brand B
Narrow or wide fit
Kids or adult
So the response follows how sure the system is

Free text keeps the number and drops the context that gives it meaning. We landed on three responses, calibrated to confidence.

Sure · 90% and up
Act quietly.

Apply the size and pin a receipt with a one-tap undo. Size 11 on Jordans applies men's 11, with women's 11 one tap away. EU 42 converts and includes US M8.5, US W10 and UK 7.5 listings.

Unsure · 50 to 89%
Ask once.

Friendly chips with the translation printed on them, and the results stay visible behind the question. Size 10 on Air Max 90 asks: men's 10 or women's 10?

Weak signal · under 50%
Educate, don't filter.

Show the full results with size guidance inline. Never guess a filter the shopper didn't give, so nothing they wanted gets hidden.

On mobile, the translation sits on the tile

The size sheet puts men's and women's sizes that fit the same on one tile, so a shopper sees M 10 and W 11.5 together instead of doing the math.

Set aside along the way: a blocking modal (it slowed experts down), silent personalization (it tested as creepy), and “fix the data first” (a multi-year wait).
04 · Worked example 2 · Local intent
Signal Exploration and tradeoffs

eBay couldn't hear “near me.”

Buyers typed “couch near me, pick up Saturday.” Search heard “couch,” sent a sectional from 800 miles away with $120 of freight, and they left for Facebook Marketplace. We tried three ways to respond.

A · Set aside
Always push local

Felt pushy. People learn to ignore the module, and then they ignore us.

B · Set aside
Always ask first

You typed “pick up Saturday,” so why is it asking? Asking about what you just said reads as not listening.

C · Shipped
Match the buyer's energy

Said it clearly: act. Maybe: a gentle offer. Didn't mean it: nothing. It plays back “here's how I read that” and searches.

07 · Why it compounds
Signal Platform thinking

One loop, both sides fed.

Buyer friction on an attribute becomes a signal back to the seller: “you may want to clarify this.” Or the marketplace just fixes it. That's the job.

Buyer gets stuck → eBay reads the signal → Seller clarifies the listing → Next buyer finds it ↺

Buying informs selling. Selling informs buying. The loop keeps turning.

08 · The zoom out · AI Mode
Signal Agentic UX

Then search learned to understand intent.

Clean data unlocked the shift from a flat results page to an agent that asks first. In AI Mode, Auto runs the same searches from the worked examples with the same spine: act when sure, ask once when unsure, and nothing is bought, bid, listed or sent without the shopper's OK.

One box, twelve modes
Each mode attaches criteria to the item: a price to wait for, a pickup window, a look, a fit. When the shopper just types, Auto picks the mode.
An agent that asks first
It asks two quick things when it has to, shows its steps, and stops before money moves.
09 · Local intent, in AI Mode
Signal Designing for trust

Nearby now: an agent that shares its prediction before it acts.

Some intent is local and fast: I need it tonight, or I'd like it sold this week. If an agent can coordinate a pickup against your calendar, local should turn on. The rule that keeps it safe: the agent says what it predicts before it acts, on both sides of the trade.

Buying agent
I need a PS5 controller tonight. Is anyone selling one nearby?
Sam can meet at 6:30 PM, about 20 minutes from you. Want me to hold it and message Sam?
Selling agent
I'd like to sell my desk chair this week. Can someone local pick it up?
Here's my prediction: list it for local pickup and a buyer could be at your door in about 30 minutes. Want me to list it?
The pickup, end to end

Payment waits for a press and hold. The exact spot stays hidden until the buyer pays, and the seller is paid when they scan the buyer's code at the handoff.

Stated up front, following through reads as the feature, not the surprise.

11 · The ideas I set aside
Signal Judgment

The two I chose not to ship.

Each thread had a bolder version. Both were ambitious, and both simplified into what shipped.

Size and search
A game board of listings

Every listing a card, and search a game board. Filters as piles, a deck you flip to tune your taste, a For You hand that fans out. Cards are a mental model every culture already shares.

Simplified into: the smart size filter and ask-once chips.
Local intent
An immersive map

Local intent as a place, not a list: everything nearby on one map you explore first, with sellers, pickup windows and prices pinned where they are.

Simplified into: a short shelf with list and map, and “Why this” on every card.
Why they stayed concepts: both were heavy builds, not the lean, near-term play. Knowing which bold idea to set aside is part of the job, and both are still buildable.
12 · Reflection
Signal Honest judgment

What started as a size fix became AI Mode.

What I'd say plainly.

The size and local work shipped in search. AI Mode is a concept built on that foundation, with sample data, and nobody has used it for real yet.

What I learned.

A simple answer needs a lot behind it. The ask-once chip only works because the seller data, the confidence rule and twelve teams were lined up behind it.

What I'd do differently.

Define the commitment and preference types before the first AI Mode build. A review found both gaps after the spec had already encoded them, and fixing a definition costs more once three flows depend on it.

What stuck.

The spine. Act when sure, ask once when unsure, educate when the signal is weak, and never move money alone. It ran from one size chip to twelve AI Mode modes without changing.

The lead lesson
Small, repeated signals are use cases. Fix them where the data starts, hold one rule across every surface, and the agent has solid ground to stand on.
