eBay AI Mode

eBay needs an AI mode → The agent does the hard work. You make the call.

eBay's best features, like Best Offer, auctions and one-of-a-kind listings, leave buyers and sellers doing a lot of checking on their own. I designed AI Mode, an agent that does the checking but never buys, bids or lists anything without the person's OK.

01 · The problem

Lots of choice. Lots of checking.

Buyers

They know what they want, but get stuck at the last step. Is it the right one? Is the seller safe? What's the total? Would an offer work? So it sits on their watchlist.

Sellers

Selling one item means a long form, a price with no guide, and low offers to answer one by one. It can feel like a second job.

The question I set
How can an agent do the hard work while the person keeps the final say?
02 · What I did

Three moves.

1
Picked what only eBay can do

I ranked 13 ideas by value to eBay and what no other store could copy. Deal hunter, built on Best Offer, came first. All 13 were for buyers, so I added selling.

2
Wrote one rule for when it acts

Not sure? It asks. Easy to undo? It acts and shows Undo. Spends money or can't be undone? It shows a preview and waits for you to press and hold.

3
Raised the bar after a review

A review found one tap could send offers to two sellers, so a shopper could owe $461 on a $250 budget. I rewrote the brief: every approval shows the full cost.

Deal hunter, in two screens

A shopper wants Air Jordan 4s in size 10.5 for under $180.

03 · The result

12 modes, and every money move waited for a tap.

The prototype runs from the eBay home page through 12 ways to buy or sell. The shopper approved 25 actions, and the agent never moved money on its own.

Said plainly
A concept with sample data. Not shipped, and not yet tested with shoppers.
What's next
Test it with 5 shoppers, check every agent claim against its proof, and run a first pilot in sneakers.
The lesson
An agent earns trust one approved decision at a time. Decide what the person must approve first, then design the agent.
