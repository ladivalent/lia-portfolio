01 · The problem

Too many apps. Too many steps.

Suppliers started from Retail Link, a page of links to more than 100 apps. Each job lived in a different app, so a normal day meant jumping between tools. Getting a new supplier started took 47 days on average.

“There are too many applications. I don't know where to find what I need to manage my business.”
Supplier interview
Why it mattered
98.5% of Walmart U.S. revenue comes through suppliers that sell to Walmart directly. When suppliers slow down, prices and sales feel it too.
02 · What I did

I built it around the supplier's work, not Walmart's tools.

I talked to suppliers, studied how Amazon, eBay, Target and Best Buy do it, and asked suppliers to sort their tasks into groups. That shaped three moves.

1
Group it by task

Suppliers think in tasks, not app names. Eight tools became five task areas, from Onboarding to Payments.

2
Show their to-dos first

Suppliers come in two types, and some are both. The home page shows each one their own top tasks, like orders that are past due.

3
Build parts every team can reuse

I built shared tables and other parts on top of Walmart's design system, so all 4 teams built screens the same way.

03 · How we shipped

Small groups first, then everyone.

I set up a simple check in Figma so developers could compare each build to the design before release. Then we opened Supplier One in three steps, so we could fix and measure along the way.
